## 4. Deploy as `stcav`, never root

Standard for this box. `git` operations in the docroot run **as `stcav`**; running them as root
leaves root-owned objects that break the next deploy.

## 5. ⚠ THE REMOTE TRAP — `origin` IS A DIFFERENT REPOSITORY

**On Windows (this machine), the push is:**

```bash
git push live feat/worksheet-classifier-universal
```

**`origin` here is `21st-Opps-Doc-Tool` — a DIFFERENT project.** A bare `git push`, or any
`git push origin …`, sends RAMS code to the wrong repository. Name the remote every time.

**Server-side the remote IS `origin`**, so the same command on the VPS is the ordinary one. The two
machines disagree about what `origin` means; that is the whole trap.

## 6. ⚠ TELL THE OFFICE: THE SITE-SURVEY CREATION FORM IS NOW THREE STEPS

A PM who knows the old single form **will think fields have gone missing.** They have not. The form
is now:

1. **Dates, contact and engineer** — survey date, surveyor, site contact, site contact phone, visit
   date, engineer → **Next**
2. **Notes** — general notes, site access notes, parking restraints, delivery routes, distance from
   base, office review notes → **Next**
3. **Spaces and output** — confirm the spaces being surveyed (**all ticked by default**), the
   Word/PDF choice, and **Generate document**

**Two things to say out loud when you tell them:**

- **Comms room is gone from the office form ON PURPOSE.** The surveyor is still asked for it **on
  site**, through the engineer link, and it still appears in the survey Word document and still
  carries forward to the installing engineer. Nothing was lost.
- **Nothing is saved until you press "Generate document".** Closing the tab on step 2 loses the
  typing. That is deliberate: it is the only way to guarantee an engineer never arrives on site
  against a half-made survey nobody finished.

And what they gain: **one press now produces the document, the visit and the engineer link
together**, and the confirmation names all three.

## 7. After deploy — the two-minute smoke test

1. Open a real project's cockpit. The **client company name** is on the masthead.
2. Site survey → **Create document**. It says **Step 1 of 3**.
3. Press **Next** twice, then the browser's **Back** button — you land on step 2 with your answers.
4. Finish it. The confirmation names the document, the visit and the engineer link.
5. **Open the engineer link.** It opens on the survey you just made, and it still asks the surveyor
   about the **comms room**.
6. Open an install's engineer link: **three** photo trays per room.

If step 2 still shows one long form, it is a stale compiled view — go back to §3.
