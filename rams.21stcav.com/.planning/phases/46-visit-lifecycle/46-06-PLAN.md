<objective>
Two of D-02's four PM actions: **accept** a returned visit, and **send it back**. Both act on one
visit, so both live on the visit's own row in the panel — not in Quick actions, which is where a
module acts.

Purpose: this is the workflow spine. Until a PM can say "yes, that's done" or "no, go back", a
returned visit is just another record that nobody turned into work.
Output: two POST actions, a visit row that shows at most four controls and only when they apply, and
a visible scope lock.
</objective>

  <behavior>
    - `POST /projects/{project}/cockpit/visits/{visit}/accept` sets `accepted_at = now()` and
      `accepted_by_user_id = auth()->id()`, writes one `ProjectActivityLog` row with
      `ACTION_VISIT_ACCEPTED`, and redirects back to `?module={key}` with a success flash.

      un-accept that silently cleared `accepted_by_user_id` would erase the record of who said yes.

      `survey_data` are byte-identical before and after. **That is D-02's "do not change what the
      engineer said", asserted rather than intended.**
    - After acceptance the panel renders "Accepted by {name} on {d M Y}" and the visit's scope edit
      affordance is GONE — replaced by the sentence "Scope locked — returned {d M Y}". Visibly
      locked, not silently disabled. A disabled control that never explains itself is how a PM
      concludes the page is broken.
    - `Visit::isClosed()` now returns true for it, so the module's progress ring counts it. A test
      asserts the ring's "n of m visits completed" sentence moves by exactly one.
